Intro

On the Green Bank Telescope at Green Bank Observatory.

I am an Astrophysics PhD student at UC Santa Barbara working with Dr. Caitlin Casey.

My research focuses on dusty star-forming galaxies, some of the most massive galaxies in the early universe. Imaging and spectroscopy across many wavelengths, from the optical and near-infrared with JWST to the (sub)millimeter, reveal their structures and star formation, offering clues to both their past and their possible futures.

Research

Dusty star-forming galaxies

My PhD research with Dr. Caitlin Casey at UC Santa Barbara focuses on dusty star-forming galaxies (DSFGs), some of the most intensely star-forming systems in the universe, whose starlight is largely hidden behind dust. Using JWST NIRCam and MIRI imaging and NIRSpec spectroscopy in the COSMOS field, I study DSFGs to characterize their structures, mass build-up, and star formation histories, piecing together their evolutionary story.

Previous work

At the Space Telescope Science Institute and Johns Hopkins University, I worked with Dr. Katherine Alatalo and Dr. Kate Rowlands on IC2520, a nearby low-mass galaxy, to understand how galaxies like it shut down their star formation (AAS 245 iPoster).

Presenting IC2520 at the JHU Summer STEM Research Symposium, 2024.

At Green Bank Observatory, I worked with Dr. Anika Schmiedeke on understanding the gas kinematics of the prestellar core L1544 to search for magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) waves (AAS 243 iPoster, NRAO report).
